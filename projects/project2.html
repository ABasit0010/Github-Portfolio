Discrete-Component Audio Amplifier

A high-fidelity audio amplifier built entirely from discrete transistors, driving an 8 Ω, 5 W speaker from a low-level microphone input.

Analog Electronics Discrete Transistors Class AB Amplifier

Abstract

Designed and implemented a high-fidelity discrete-component audio amplifier capable of driving an 8 Ω, 5 W speaker from a low-level microphone input. Built entirely from discrete transistors rather than integrated op-amps, the circuit delivers clean, powerful audio output while reinforcing fundamentals of small-signal and power amplifier design.

Architecture

  • Preamplifier (3 stages): a Common Collector buffer for high input impedance and microphone matching, followed by two Common Emitter stages with voltage gains Av1 ≈ 23 and Av2 ≈ 22, giving an overall gain Av ≈ 506.
  • Output stage — Class AB amplifier: push–pull configuration using an NPN TIP41 and PNP TIP42, quiescent current set for minimal crossover distortion, and large aluminum heat sinks for thermal stability.

Key Specifications

  • Input signal: 8 mV peak (mic)
  • Output signal: ±4 Vrms
  • Load: 8 Ω, 5 W speaker
  • Supply voltage: ±12 V
  • Total harmonic distortion (THD): < 5%
  • Input impedance: ~100 kΩ
  • Power output: ~3 W

Features

  • High input impedance for optimal microphone performance.
  • High voltage and power gain to efficiently drive speakers.
  • Low noise and distortion for clear audio reproduction.
  • Thermal stability with robust heat dissipation.

Applications

Ideal for small-scale PA systems, educational electronics projects, and embedded audio solutions.
