Closed Loop Buck Converter (50–100 W)

Abstract

This project presents the design, modeling, simulation, and hardware implementation of a closed-loop buck converter capable of delivering 50–100 W output power. Feedback control using a PI/PID-based controller ensures stable voltage regulation under varying load conditions. Mathematical modeling and transfer function analysis guided the controller design, while Proteus and MATLAB simulations validated system performance. Experimental hardware testing confirmed excellent voltage regulation, low ripple, and high efficiency.

Objectives

  • Design a closed-loop buck converter with 50–100 W output power.
  • Develop mathematical and small-signal models of the system.
  • Design a stable feedback controller with zero steady-state error.
  • Validate performance through simulation and hardware testing.
  • Perform loss analysis to identify dominant power losses.

Hardware Components

  • DC Input Power Supply (20 V)
  • IRFZ44 MOSFET
  • MBR30CHT20 Fast Recovery Diode
  • Inductor: 1 mH, 5 A
  • Capacitor: 250 µF, 200 V
  • Arduino (PWM & Control)
  • Resistive Load Bank

System Design & Functionality

The buck converter operates in continuous conduction mode (CCM) and steps down a 20 V DC input to a regulated 10 V output. A closed-loop feedback system senses output voltage via a resistor divider and compares it with a reference voltage. A PID-based controller adjusts the PWM duty cycle to maintain regulation under load and input variations. Switching frequency is set to 20 kHz.

Technical Specifications

  • Input Voltage: 20 V DC
  • Output Voltage: 10 V DC (regulated)
  • Output Power: 50–100 W
  • Switching Frequency: 20 kHz
  • Control Method: Closed-loop PID (Arduino)
  • Operating Mode: Continuous Conduction Mode (CCM)

Control & Mathematical Modeling

A small-signal averaged model of the buck converter was derived to obtain the control-to-output transfer function. The plant exhibits a second-order LC response. A PID (Type-III) compensator was analytically designed to achieve sufficient phase margin, fast transient response, and zero steady-state error.

Key Transfer Function

// Buck Converter Control-to-Output Transfer Function
Gvd(s) = Vin * (rC*C*s + 1) /
         (L*C*s^2 + (L/R + rL*C + rC*C)*s + (1 + rL/R))

Simulation & Testing Results

MATLAB and Proteus simulations confirmed stable closed-loop operation with a crossover frequency of approximately 100 Hz and phase margin exceeding 55°. Hardware testing demonstrated low output ripple, fast transient response, and reliable regulation under load changes. The converter successfully powered high-current resistive loads within supply limitations.

Conclusion

The closed-loop buck converter was successfully designed, simulated, and implemented. Feedback control significantly improved voltage regulation, stability, and transient performance compared to open-loop operation. Loss analysis provided insights into efficiency optimization, making the design suitable for real-world power electronics applications.
